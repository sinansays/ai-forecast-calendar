# Additional forecast source reviews

**Review date:** 2026-08-13

This record explains what was included in the first multi-source release and what was withheld. Calendar dates are presentation anchors; they do not upgrade an author's approximate date or a probability threshold into an exact-day claim.

## Included: Situational Awareness

- **Primary source:** [*Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead*](https://situational-awareness.ai/), Leopold Aschenbrenner, June 2024.
- **Why it qualifies:** named author, stable long-form publication, many explicit year/range anchors, and separable capability, compute, investment, governance, and intelligence-explosion claims.
- **Editorial scope:** 14 milestones were selected. Repeated statements of the same AGI, cluster, or superintelligence claim were merged. Undated policy recommendations and relative-only claims were excluded.
- **Caveat:** this is a scenario/argument with order-of-magnitude extrapolations, not a calibrated event-by-event forecast.

## Included: Grace et al. 2023 expert survey

- **Primary source:** [*Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843), Grace et al., reporting a survey of 2,778 AI researchers.
- **Why it qualifies:** identifiable research authorship, versioned paper, explicit aggregate probability years, published definitions, and a method distinct from narrative scenarios.
- **Editorial scope:** only four headline 10% and 50% thresholds for high-level machine intelligence and full automation of labor are included. The events describe cumulative survey thresholds, not four separate predicted arrivals.
- **Caveat:** survey aggregation and the paper's definitions are essential context. July 1 anchors year-only results.

## Included: Peter Wildeford's AGI Timelines Model

- **Primary source:** [Peter Wildeford's `agi_timelines` model](https://github.com/peterhurford/agi_timelines), originally published April 2025; snapshot reviewed at the repository's 2026-02-09 commit `664e1cec269c49534e3a59860e1f096b7b6b7b2e`.
- **Why it qualifies:** accountable author, public model and assumptions, dated version history, explicit probabilistic output, and a quantitative method based on METR task-horizon trends.
- **Editorial scope:** one frozen 100,000-sample default-model extraction is represented at its 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles. These are cumulative distribution crossings, not separate AGI events.
- **Caveat:** results are assumption-sensitive and the upstream repository evolves. This calendar must remain a snapshot rather than silently follow new commits.

## Included: AI 2040 — Plan A

- **Primary source:** [*AI 2040: Plan A*](https://ai-2040.com/), Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, and Daniel Kokotajlo of the AI Futures Project, July 2026.
- **Why it qualifies:** named authors, a published PDF and interactive scenario, explicit annual chapters from 2027 through 2040, and direct statements separating recommendations from conditional predictions.
- **Editorial scope:** one conceptual milestone per scenario year captures the policy transition, capability progression, economic transformation, safety work, and eventual handoff to aligned superintelligence. July 1 anchors year-level chapters; the training halt uses the end of 2029 because the source says it lasts for the rest of that year; the 2040 point of no return uses October 25 as a transparent representative date for “late October.”
- **Caveat:** Plan A is primarily a policy recommendation and explicitly not the authors' best guess. The calendar therefore labels the downstream events as conditional on successful implementation rather than presenting the whole sequence as an unconditional forecast.
